# Agents propose, founders approve, history remembers

State-of-the-art CRMs in late 2026 share one shape, and CRM System can only reach it by rebuilding its memory before its screens. On the surface, Attio, Folk, Twenty and Close have converged on the same layout: a quiet left sidebar of objects, lists and favorites; saved table and kanban views over the same records; a two-pane record page with a capped highlight strip, a tabbed timeline and a details sidebar, also reachable through a roughly 500px peek panel; and ⌘K plus single-key shortcuts, all at Twenty-like density (13px Inter, 32px rows, 16px icons). HubSpot's refresh, mandatory since 31 August 2026, and Salesforce's Cosmos theme now chase the same restraint. Underneath, the leaders run on typed records keyed by normalized email and domain, upserts that refuse ambiguous matches, append-only value and stage history (which feeds rotting signals, time-in-stage, funnels and field history), and a restorable trash. AI participation has settled into risk tiers. Agents write notes, summaries and agent-owned AI fields freely. Stage, amount, close date and merges arrive instead as current-versus-proposed diffs with an evidence quote, for a human to accept, edit or reject (Affinity, HubSpot's Closing Agent, Pipedrive Nova, Lightfield). External agents enter through hosted MCP servers that act with the user's permissions. Two gaps are open across the whole market: no vendor MCP server offers a server-side "propose" operation, and no vendor documents undo for agent work. That makes enforced proposals, batch undo and reversible merges the clearest differentiators for a local app that a person and an agent run together. CRM System has the right screens but not that memory. Its ops manifest exposes six operations, and none of them creates, searches, merges or undoes a record. Its schema has no actor, history, soft-delete, currency, deal-status or stage-probability fields, and notes and activities are hard-deleted along with their parent record. The redesign should therefore start with an actor-stamped event log, versioned values, stage events, proposals and batch undo exposed as ops, and only then rebuild the interface to Attio/Twenty density, with a Focus queue where agent proposals wait for one-key review.

## One quiet, keyboard-first shell has won, and the incumbents are copying it

The modern leaders differ in data model and price, but by late 2026 they all use the same shell. Attio's sidebar starts with a control panel and a search block (`cmd + k` for commands, `/` for search). Below that is a navigation panel ("Home page, notifications inbox, tasks, notes, emails, reports, sequences, and workflows"), then a Records section listing every object, a Lists section, and a Chats section for its AI assistant ([Attio: Navigating Attio](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/navigating-attio)). Favorites and folders are personal to each user, and the sidebar shows **six lists by default** so it stays short ([Attio: Navigating your workspace](https://attio.com/help/reference/tips-and-tricks/quick-actions)). Twenty, the open-source analogue, lets users drag-reorder the sidebar, group objects and views into folders, hide unused objects, and pin views, records or searches as favorites ([Twenty docs: layout](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/layout/overview)). The incumbents have conceded the point. In its July 2026 rollout, Zoho moved its modules from a top bar into "one unified sidebar" and centered search at the top ([Zoho UI comparison](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/crm-v2-1/using-crm-for-everyone/transition-guide/articles/zoho-crm-ui-comparison-31-7-2026)). HubSpot's 2026 refresh kept its navigation but stripped visual weight from almost everything else ([Vantagepoint](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/hs/hubspot-2026-redesign-what-to-know)).

Inside that shell, every leader treats tables, boards and (in Twenty) calendars as **saved, named views over one dataset**. Each view keeps its own filters, sorts, visible fields and grouping ([Twenty docs: layout](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/layout/overview)). Folk adds a twist that suits founders: records are workspace-wide, but **groups** such as Leads, Investors, Clients or Candidates act as per-workflow databases. A record can sit in many groups, and custom fields belong to one group, so each workflow's table carries only its own columns ([folk: data model](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/9790806-folk-data-model)).

The best tables behave like spreadsheets. Attio supports multi-cell selection by click-drag or shift-click, **copy and paste to and from external spreadsheets**, arrow-key navigation with Enter to save, relative dates ("in 3 days") and a "+ Add calculation" footer on every column ([Attio: table views](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/views/create-and-manage-table-views)). Twenty's footer offers twelve aggregate operations, including count empty, percent empty and count unique ([Twenty source: AggregateOperations.ts](https://github.com/twentyhq/twenty/blob/main/packages/twenty-front/src/modules/object-record/record-table/constants/AggregateOperations.ts)), and its docs advise keeping grouped tables to 10-15 visible groups ([Twenty docs: table views](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/views-pipelines/capabilities/table-views)). HubSpot's index-page redesign went to public beta on **27 May 2026** and became mandatory in late July. It moved view settings, a collapsible filter bar, a strip of summary metrics and Breeze AI column insights onto the list page itself ([Vantagepoint: index redesign](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/hs/hubspot-smart-crm-index-redesign-guide)).

Boards follow a shared grammar: a stage header with a color dot, the stage name, a count and an aggregate; compact cards with three to five configurable fields; and a staleness signal. In Attio, "Track time in stage" takes a target and turns the card's counter **red** once the deal exceeds it; collapsed columns keep only the stage color, name and count; several cards drag at once; hovering a card offers "Open preview"; and an optional per-stage confetti burst marks wins ([Attio: kanban views](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/views/create-and-manage-kanban-views)). Pipedrive cards show title, contact, value, label, owner and an activity icon, and deals are sorted by next activity. A **"Rotting"** highlight flags neglected deals, and **drop zones at the bottom of the board** mark a deal won, lost or deleted ([Pipedrive: pipeline view](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/pipeline-view)). Salesforce sums Amount at the top of each column and recalculates the total live during a drag ([Trailhead: Kanban](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/projects/customize-a-sales-path-for-your-team/work-with-opportunities-in-the-kanban-view)). None of the CRMs researched offers WIP limits; time-in-stage targets and rotting do that job.

### The record page settles on two panes, a capped highlight strip and a peek panel

Record pages have converged too. Attio's Overview tab holds up to **six highlight widgets**, and the default tabs are Overview, Activity, Notes, Tasks, Emails and Files. A mandatory Record Details sidebar shows the first five attributes and the lists the record belongs to ([Attio: configure record pages](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/records/configure-record-pages)). HubSpot's 2026 default is a two-column page: a header with **up to five properties**, an avatar, two quick-activity buttons and previous/next arrows; tabs for Overview (AI summary and Health), Activities, Revenue and Intelligence; and a right sidebar with inline-editable key information, associations shown as a list or as chips, and attachments with a "Focused" filter ([HubSpot KB: default record layout](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/understand-the-default-record-layout)). Salesforce's highlights panel holds up to ten fields and shows seven, and the same compact layout feeds hover cards and timeline expansions ([Salesforce docs: compact layouts](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.210.0.salesforce1appadmin.meta/salesforce1appadmin/s1_admin_guide_compact_layouts_intro.htm)). Pipedrive's deal page adds two devices worth copying. A stage progress bar shows **days spent in each stage**. A **Focus** section of open activities, drafts and pinned notes sits above an immutable History, and a separate Changelog tab sits alongside ([Pipedrive: deal detail view](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/deal-detail-view)). Close's redesigned lead page scrolls details and feed independently ("split scrolling") and is reported to load 40% faster ([Close changelog](https://www.close.com/changelog/july-2024-improvements)). Since August 2025, Close also keeps pinned notes at the top of the feed ([Close: pinned notes](https://www.close.com/changelog/pinned-notes-and-custom-activities)).

Opening a record quickly matters as much as the full page. In Twenty, a click opens a **500px right side panel**, and "Open" leads to a full page of resizable widgets ([Twenty docs: layout](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/layout/overview); [Twenty ThemeCommon.ts](https://github.com/twentyhq/twenty/blob/main/packages/twenty-ui/src/theme/constants/ThemeCommon.ts)). Folk's "side peek" and Attio's "Open preview" on kanban cards do the same job ([folk changelog](https://www.folk.app/changelog)). Reviewers punish the alternatives. Pipedrive's left-aligned detail redesign wasted space and drew complaints that "all information is concentrated on the left side leaving half the window empty" ([Pipedrive community](https://community.pipedrive.com/discussion/12010/new-details-screen-for-deals)). Salesforce is called "cluttered and counterintuitive", with "more clicks than it should" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/61368/Salesforce/reviews/?page=49)). Folk loses points because it keeps tasks, notes and reports "more hidden behind the core spreadsheet view" ([Whalesync](https://www.whalesync.com/blog/attio-vs-folk)). The best-supported layout is therefore two panes, each scrolling on its own: a tabbed main column and a narrow details sidebar. Add a highlight strip capped at five to seven fields, and a peek panel from every table and board. Pipedrive's Focus-above-History split is the natural home for an agent's pending proposals.

Attio moved the same way in August 2026, when it standardized **icons and avatars at 16px** across the product ([Attio changelog, 25 Aug 2026](https://attio.com/changelog/2026/changelog-august-25-2026)). HubSpot's refresh was opt-in from **30 June 2026** and mandatory from **31 August 2026**. It promises "lighter typography, more intentional spacing, and less reliance on heavy headers" ([Vantagepoint](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/hs/hubspot-2026-redesign-what-to-know)). Salesforce's Cosmos theme makes shape carry meaning: rounded corners are interactive and square corners are not. It also adds drop shadows and gradients to buttons, which is the opposite of restraint ([Trailhead: Enhanced Lightning UI](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/enhanced-lightning-ui-quick-look/discover-enhanced-lightning-ui)).

Restraint has a floor, though. Marmelab rates Twenty the best open-source CRM (9/10) yet calls its "list UI ... bland", and it marks Krayin down for "low information density" ([Marmelab 2026](https://marmelab.com/blog/2026/01/09/open-source-crm-benchmark-2026.html)). Quiet tables still need visual hierarchy: avatars, colored status chips, stage dots and relative dates, with color reserved for those elements.

Speed is the other half of the feeling. A 2026 review found that Attio filters "20k+ records ... within milliseconds with no lag" ([Hackceleration](https://www.hackceleration.com/labs/review/attio)), and Attio's September 2026 release made iOS record and table views "load ten times faster" ([Attio changelog, 23 Sep 2026](https://attio.com/changelog/2026/changelog-september-23-2026)). Keyboard depth separates the leaders from HubSpot. Close publishes a full map: `?` for the cheat sheet, ⌘K for the command palette, ⌘⇧F for search, J/K to move through lists, Enter to open, X to check, single-key actions in the inbox and `/` for quick insert in the editor ([Close: keyboard shortcuts](https://help.close.com/docs/keyboard-shortcuts)). Attio adds the single keys n, t and c for a new note, task or email, more than 30 quick actions, and a ⌘K menu whose suggestions change with context and include today's meetings ([Attio: quick actions](https://attio.com/help/reference/tips-and-tricks/quick-actions)). The last ingredient is never showing a blank canvas. Even so, one team "didn't do anything on it for three months because we didn't know how to build lists" ([Hackceleration](https://www.hackceleration.com/labs/review/attio)).

## Typed records keyed on email and domain make imports, dedupe and merges safe

The best-practice data model is metadata-driven, and Attio documents it most completely. **Objects** (people, companies, optional deals and custom objects) hold **records**. **Lists** model processes: each list entry wraps one record and carries list-specific attributes. The same record can appear twice in a list, for example a person applying for two roles, and each entry keeps its own stage and hiring manager ([Attio: objects and lists](https://docs.attio.com/docs/objects-and-lists.md); [Attio: understanding lists](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/attios-data-model/understanding-lists)). Attio has **17 attribute types**, and every stored value carries `active_from` and `active_until`, so history is built into the data rather than added later ([Attio: attribute types](https://docs.attio.com/docs/attribute-types)). Twenty's metadata enum has 25 field types, including ACTOR, LINKS, polymorphic MORPH_RELATION, RICH_TEXT and TS_VECTOR for full-text search ([Twenty FieldMetadataType.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twentyhq/twenty/main/packages/twenty-shared/src/types/FieldMetadataType.ts)).

Typed values do the normalization so that callers don't have to. A phone number is stored as the original input, an ISO country code and an E.164-normalized number ([Attio: phone](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/attribute-types/attribute-types-phone-number.md)). A domain is stored with its root domain, with paths and query strings trimmed ([Attio: domain](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/attribute-types/attribute-types-domain.md)). Each status option carries a target time-in-status and a celebration flag, and writing an unknown stage title returns an error rather than creating a new stage ([Attio: status](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/attribute-types/attribute-types-status.md)). Record references come in two-way pairs, so adding a person to a company's team automatically updates that person's company ([Attio: record reference](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/attribute-types/attribute-types-record-reference.md)).

Relationships need roles, and the counter-example is instructive. HubSpot labels associations, for example a Primary company plus "Branch" companies, or a contact labeled "Decision Maker" ([HubSpot: associations](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api-reference/latest/crm/associations/associations-schema/guide)). Pipedrive allows **one linked contact per deal**. Additional people become "participants", who "won't appear in the list view or Insights reports" ([Pipedrive: participants](https://support.pipedrive.com/article/participants)). Deals and people should therefore be many-to-many, with a role on each link.

Founders also run several processes over the same people. Streak runs "sales, hiring, investor outreach, fundraising, partnerships" as separate pipelines in one system ([G2: Streak](https://g2.com/products/streak/reviews?page=3)). Attio's VC guide models roles such as "Lead Investor, Board Observer, or Warm Intro Source" as relationship attributes, and keeps lists for "one-off processes" ([Attio: VC guide](https://attio.com/help/reference/industry-guides/vc.md)). The model that fits CRM System is shared People and Companies, several deal pipelines (Sales, Fundraising, Hiring, Partnerships), and lists for lighter processes such as Community.

Identity rests on natural keys. HubSpot deduplicates contacts on **email**, companies on **domain** and everything else on record ID, and it allows up to ten custom unique properties ([HubSpot KB: deduplication](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/deduplication-of-records)). Attio's upsert takes a matching attribute: a value that matches more than one record is **rejected**, a value that matches nothing creates a new record, and when the match key is multi-value (such as emails) new values are appended while existing ones are kept ([Attio: upsert a record](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/endpoint-reference/records/upsert-a-record.md)). Creating a person in Attio also creates or matches their company from the email domain ([Attio: People object](https://docs.attio.com/docs/standard-objects/standard-objects-people)). Its communication sync can be limited to domains you have emailed, so newsletters and spam never become records ([Attio: communication intelligence](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/productivity/communications-intelligence)).

One HubSpot detail carries a warning for an agent-operated app: companies created through the API "will not be deduplicated by the Company domain name property" ([HubSpot KB: deduplication](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/deduplication-of-records)). In CRM System, the API is the agent's main write path, so dedupe has to live in the API rather than only in the UI.

Import, duplicate detection and merge all have well-documented rules to copy. HubSpot's import auto-maps columns using "the header, the header language, and the property name". It matches on the same unique identifiers. If a user asks to "update existing deals" without a Record ID column, they must fix the file and restart ([HubSpot KB: import tool](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/import-and-export/understand-the-import-tool)). Salesforce's standard contact rule matches email **exactly** and first and last names **fuzzily**. It catches "Bob" versus "Robert" but misses the same person under a different email ([Trailhead: duplicate management](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/sales_admin_duplicate_management/sales_admin_duplicate_management_unit_2)). HubSpot's duplicate tool compares records daily and offers pairs to merge or reject, and its automatic merge covers exact one-to-one matches only ([HubSpot KB: manage duplicates](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/manage-duplicate-records)).

HubSpot's merge precedence is the most complete published rule set. The primary record wins and its blank fields are filled from the secondary; the secondary's email and domain survive as secondary values; lifecycle keeps the furthest stage and create date the oldest value; timelines and associations are combined; and the merged record gets a new ID, because "It's not possible to unmerge records" ([HubSpot KB: merge records](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/merge-records)). Pipedrive keeps the primary's values with no per-field choice ([Pipedrive KB: merge duplicates](https://support.pipedrive.com/th/article/merge-duplicates)).

CRM System should copy HubSpot's precedence rules but fix the irreversibility. It should store the secondary record's snapshot and map the old ID to the survivor, so a merge can be reversed and a stale agent reference still resolves. Attio's MCP server exposes `merge-records` with the tightest rate limit of any write ([Attio MCP docs](https://docs.attio.com/mcp/overview)), which confirms that merging is the riskiest operation an agent gets. For logos, note that the **Clearbit Logo API shut down on 8 December 2025**. Logo.dev, built by the original team, is the drop-in successor with a free tier of 500K requests a month ([Logo.dev](https://www.logo.dev/clearbit)). A local app can use it and fall back to the company's initials.

## Stage history and interaction history drive pipelines, follow-ups and relationship scores

### Pipelines need probabilities, required won and lost stages, and a rotting clock

A modern pipeline is more than ordered columns. HubSpot assigns each stage a win probability (its defaults run from 20% at Appointment scheduled to 90% at Contract sent) and **requires both a Won and a Lost stage**. It can require certain properties before a deal may enter a stage. It blocks deleting a stage until the deals in it are moved ([HubSpot KB: pipelines](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/object-settings/set-up-and-customize-pipelines)). Moving a deal updates its probability automatically, and editing the probability by hand switches that automation off. Weighted amount is "the Amount multiplied by the Deal probability". Close date is set automatically when a deal closes. Forecast categories run Not forecasted, Pipeline, Best case, Commit, Closed won ([HubSpot KB: default deal properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-deal-properties)). Pipedrive uses free-text lost reasons by default and allows up to **100 predefined reasons** ([Pipedrive KB: lost reasons](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/lost-reasons)). Its `first_won_time` stays fixed when a deal is reopened, so reports can choose between the first outcome and the latest one ([Saras Analytics: Pipedrive fields](https://docs.sarasanalytics.com/integrations/data-sources/saas-integrations/pipedrive)).

Stage history underpins the rest. For every stage, HubSpot records the date a deal entered and exited, its latest and cumulative time there, and its time in the current stage, and it fills these in retroactively from existing data ([HubSpot KB: stage-calculated properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/stage-calculated-properties)).

Pipedrive's rotting rule is the best-known stale-deal mechanic. Each stage sets "Rotting in (days)". Completed activities, notes, files, emails and field edits all reset the clock, and a rotten deal shows a red tile. The rule also "disregards the next activity date", so a deal with a meeting booked months out can still rot ([Pipedrive KB: rotting](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/the-rotting-feature)). Pipedrive also shows each deal's next-activity state as an icon and sorts each column by urgency: a red arrow for an overdue activity, green for one due today, a **yellow warning when nothing is scheduled**, and grey for a future activity ([Pipedrive KB: colored icons](https://support.pipedrive.com/hc/en-us/articles/115001107229-What-are-the-colored-icons-on-the-deals-in-the-Pipeline-View-)). Currency is where incumbents still fail. Pipedrive's reports convert values at live rates, and "the currency conversion continues to be updated after a deal is closed", so won revenue drifts. Users have asked for rates frozen at close, and Pipedrive has not built it ([Pipedrive KB: currency](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-is-currency-converted-in-pipedrive)). CRM System should store a currency on each deal, freeze the converted value when the deal closes, and never add raw amounts in different currencies together.

### The timeline and a single inbox decide who to contact today

Every leader shows a reverse-chronological timeline on each record. It merges emails, meetings, calls, notes, tasks and system events such as stage moves. The rules for what counts as contact are subtle. HubSpot's **Last activity date** includes notes and completed tasks, but **Last contacted** counts only calls, meetings, chats, messages and one-to-one emails that were not forwarded, and **Number of times contacted** excludes tasks and notes ([HubSpot KB: default deal properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/hubspots-default-deal-properties)). Pipedrive links an email to a deal automatically only if it is that person's only open deal ([Pipedrive KB: Smart BCC](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/smart-email-bcc)). HubSpot allows one pinned activity per record ([HubSpot KB: pin a note](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/contacts/pin-a-note-in-a-record)). Attio tasks are plain text of up to 2,000 characters with a deadline, linked records and assignees. Assignees are actor references, so a task can belong to a non-human actor ([Attio: create a task](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/endpoint-reference/tasks/create-a-task.md)).

The daily work queue is an inbox. Close splits it into Inbox, Done and Future. Snoozed items go to Future. The inbox mixes due tasks, inbound replies, missed calls, potential contacts, and "opportunities on their expected closing date" ([Close Help: inbox](https://help.close.com/docs/inbox)). HubSpot's Sales Workspace puts tasks first, switchable between overdue, due today and due tomorrow, above a simplified pipeline and today's schedule ([HubSpot KB: sales workspace](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/prospecting/review-sales-activity-in-the-sales-workspace)).

Founder and personal CRMs add keep-in-touch cadences, and two vendors' semantics differ in a way that matters. Folk's reminders recur on fixed dates, fire at 9:00 AM, and "are not affected if you have an interaction with the contact before the set date" ([Folk Help: reminders](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/5658439-set-reminders-on-your-contacts)). Dex resets the next date to **last interaction plus frequency** ([Dex: keep-in-touch](https://guide.getdex.com/features/keep-in-touch)), which is what users expect: one feature request asks that the cadence "start from last interaction, not the time I set the frequency" ([Dex feedback](https://feedback.getdex.com/p/catch-up-by-date-calculation-should-start-from-last-interaction)). Clay's personal CRM shows an **Upcoming** view of "birthdays, reminders, and events for the next 30 days" ([Dex: Dex vs Clay](https://getdex.com/blog/dex-vs-clay)). Monica's main weakness, according to a review by its competitor Dex, is that it "requires you to manually enter every interaction" ([Dex: Monica review](https://getdex.com/blog/monica-review/)). CRM System has no email sync, so one-keystroke interaction logging matters even more. A founder guide suggests tracking five things per contact: "who they are, how you know them, last contact date, next action, and relevant notes". It also advises starting with about 50 people rather than the whole network ([River](https://rivereditor.com/blogs/personal-crm-for-founders)). The most common small-business complaint in one collection of Reddit posts is having to "click through multiple screens to see contact interaction history instead of viewing a timeline on the contact card" ([Substack](https://mihais7.substack.com/p/the-billion-dollar-complaint-a-single)).

### Relationship strength rewards recency, and intro paths take the weaker hop

Auto-computed interaction fields are now the baseline. Attio derives first, last and next interaction, overall and separately for email and calendar. It also computes a **Connection strength** by "weighting the recency and frequency of the communication", and a **Strongest connection** naming the teammate who knows the person best. It publishes no formula ([Attio: communication intelligence](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-101/productivity/communications-intelligence)). Affinity scores relationships from 10 to 100 and shows **green, orange or gray bars** for strong, average and weak. Each profile has a Connections tab that separates people you know, people your allies know, people added manually, and inferred links ([Affinity support](https://support.affinity.co/s/article/Leveraging-your-Connections-and-Relationship-Strengths)).

Village publishes a concrete scheme. Scores of 7+ are Excellent, 3-7 Good, 1-3 Okay, and below 1 Weak. A meeting adds 1 point and a recurring meeting adds 10. Meetings within the past year get a 30% bonus, and "friendly" meetings such as coffee get 70%. For a second-degree intro path, "the score is capped at the weaker of the two relationships" ([Village: how we score connections](https://docs.village.ai/help-center/building-your-network/how-we-score-connections)).

A local, single-user app has no network-wide data. It can still compute strength from logged interactions with a time decay, store explicit "knows" links between people, and rank intro paths by their weaker hop. It should record employment as history rather than a single company field, so that a past champion who changes jobs becomes a warm lead at the new company.

### Value history, dynamic views and a 90-day trash complete the memory

Attio's filter grammar is a JSON tree with `$and`, `$or` and `$not` and no `$neq`. Operators include `$eq`, `$contains`, `$starts_with`, `$in`, `$not_empty` and the range comparisons. Path filters traverse relationships, for example entries whose candidate's email domain is apple.com ([Attio: filtering and sorting](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/guides/filtering-and-sorting.md)). Status filters can ask *when* a value changed, for example "moved to stage X since date" ([Attio: status](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/attribute-types/attribute-types-status.md)). Close's Smart Views are saved filters that records flow in and out of as data changes. They support activity conditions such as "has not been contacted in X days", and Close recommends chaining them from untouched leads through attempted and cold to warm ([Close Help: Smart Views](https://help.close.com/docs/smart-views)). A grammar the agent already knows from Attio, plus relative dates and activity predicates, serves both the UI's filter chips and the ops API.

History and recovery are where the leaders' gaps begin. Attio returns every historic value of an attribute, oldest first, each with `active_from`, `active_until` and `created_by_actor`. The actor can be a workspace member, an API token, the system or an app ([Attio: list record attribute values](https://docs.attio.com/rest-api/endpoint-reference/records/list-record-attribute-values.md)). HubSpot restores deleted records for **90 days**, up to 10,000 at a time, but blocks the restore if a deleted contact's email now belongs to an active contact, and GDPR deletions are immediate and permanent ([HubSpot KB: restore deleted records](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/restore-deleted-records)). Deleted activities can also be restored within 90 days ([HubSpot KB: restore activity](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/restore-deleted-activity-in-a-record)). Pipedrive admits that some actions reset the rotting clock but "may not show up clearly in the deal changelog" ([Pipedrive KB: rotting](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/the-rotting-feature)). A change the user cannot see is the trust failure an agent-operated CRM cannot afford. None of the vendors researched documents batch undo for edits, imports or agent runs. That is the gap CRM System should fill: every mutating call returns a batch ID, a ten-second "Undo" toast appears in the UI, and the agent can call `undo` on its own batches.

## Dashboards answer three questions, and stage history pays for every chart

The best products separate a personal **"my day" home** from an **analytics dashboard**. Attio's recommended sales dashboard has five reports in a deliberate order: deals currently in each stage, a stage-to-stage funnel, win rate, average time to close, and deals moving into each stage over time ([Attio: build a sales reporting dashboard](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/dashboard-and-reports/build-a-sales-reporting-dashboard)). Pipedrive's report families cover performance, conversion, duration ("how long deals spend in each stage to identify bottlenecks"), progress, forecast and activities ([Pipedrive: Insights report types](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/insights-report-types)). A third-party Salesforce dashboard guide advises pairing each big number "with a comparison to the prior period for context". It says to use gauges only when there is a real target, and to keep a dashboard to 10-15 components ([Spekit](https://spekit.com/salesforce-lightning-dashboards)). For one person, that means a strip of five or six tiles (open pipeline, weighted forecast, won this period, win rate, average deal size and sales cycle), each with a delta against the previous period. Overdue tasks, stale deals and keep-in-touch reminders that are due belong in an action list beside the tiles, not in charts.

Chart choice follows the question being asked, and vendors are consistent about it:

| Question | Chart | Rule worth copying | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is in the pipeline now? | Horizontal bars by stage, count plus value | Horizontal bars "handle long labels better" | [Spekit](https://spekit.com/salesforce-lightning-dashboards) |
| Where do deals drop out? | Columns with "% reached" per stage, overall win rate in the header | First entry only; skipped stages count as passed; won deals pass every stage; a pipeline filter is required | [Pipedrive: deal conversion](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/insights-reports-deal-conversion) |
| What revenue is coming? | Monthly columns, cumulative by default | Won deals placed by won date, open deals by expected close; value or weighted value | [Pipedrive: revenue forecast](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/insights-reports-revenue-forecast) |
| Where does it stall? | Bars of max/average/min days per stage | Optional color breakdown by owner or category | [Attio: time in stage](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/dashboard-and-reports/set-up-a-time-in-stage-report) |
| Where do deals come from? | Donut capped at 5-6 slices, or ranked bars | Merge small slices into "Other" | [Spekit](https://spekit.com/salesforce-lightning-dashboards) |

Drill-down is now expected even in lightweight tools. Pipedrive places the chart above a table that toggles between "Deals" and "Summary" ([Pipedrive: deal conversion](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/insights-reports-deal-conversion)). Attio lets users drill into "contributing records from grouped data points" and export CSV ([Attio: create reports](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/dashboard-and-reports/reports.md)). HubSpot generates date-range and owner quick filters from the reports on a dashboard ([HubSpot KB: dashboard filters](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/dashboards/use-dashboard-filters)). NocoBase's CRM solution added click-through from charts and KPI cards in March 2026 ([NocoBase changelog](https://docs.nocobase.com/solution/crm/changelog)).

Formulas need to be visible, because vendors disagree about them. Salesforce defines win rate as won divided by all opportunities ([Salesforce: win rate](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/win-rate/)). RevOps glossaries use won divided by won plus lost over closed deals ([Count: win rate](https://count.co/metric/win-rate)). Salesforce's sales velocity is opportunities × average deal value × win rate ÷ cycle length. Its worked example of 150 × $1,000 × 0.25 ÷ 30 days gives **$1,250 a day** ([Salesforce: sales velocity](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sales-velocity/)). Every tile should show its definition in a tooltip.

Complaints about reporting come from both ends of the market. Heavy CRMs are "built for sales managers to monitor reps rather than for reps to close deals, with friction designed to generate reports no one reads" ([Substack](https://mihais7.substack.com/p/the-billion-dollar-complaint-a-single)). HubSpot users have spent at least five pages of an ideas thread asking for date filters on cross-object reports ([HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/HubSpot-Ideas/Dashboard-cannot-use-date-filter-when-using-cross-reporting/idi-p/215775/highlight/true/page/5)). SuiteCRM is marked down for "Clunky Reports & Charts" ([Marmelab 2025](https://marmelab.com/blog/2025/02/03/open-source-crm-benchmark-for-2025.html)). At the light end, Streak's G2 reviewers find its "inadequate analytics restricting" ([G2: Streak](https://g2.com/products/streak/reviews?page=3)), and folk's dashboards show "all-time data" only, with no export ([folk Help: dashboards](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/9867508-dashboards-beta)). Attio puts funnel reports behind its Plus plan, and time-in-stage, historical values and stage-change reports behind Pro or Enterprise ([Attio: create reports](https://attio.com/help/reference/managing-your-data/dashboard-and-reports/reports.md)).

Every one of those paywalled reports can be computed from a stage-event table. A local app that records stage transitions from day one gets them for free. The resulting design is six opinionated preset reports with a period filter, a pipeline filter and click-through to records, and no report builder. It answers Capsule's single small-business question: "How are we doing?" ([Capsule](https://capsulecrm.com/blog/build-your-own-dashboard/)). Fundraising pipelines need "committed versus target" rather than a weighted forecast, which is one of the few places a gauge earns its space.

## Agents propose and humans approve, but no vendor enforces it on the server

### Six jobs are automated almost everywhere

By late 2026 every major CRM ships agents for six jobs: enrichment and web research written into fields, capture of emails and meetings as notes and summaries, field updates suggested from calls, drafted follow-ups and outreach, natural-language questions over CRM data, and scheduled digests and data-hygiene sweeps. The AI-native CRMs push furthest. Lightfield reached general availability in November 2025. It builds the CRM from emails, transcripts, calls and Slack messages, and answers questions "with citations to the actual conversations" ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/saastr-ai-app-of-the-week-lightfield-the-ai-native-crm-that-killed-tomes-25-million-users-to-build-something-better/); [Lightfield changelog](https://lightfield.com/blog/suggested-record-updates-support-in-lightfield)). Day.ai turns conversations into a "context graph" and drafts emails "ready for human edit and send". It ships an API, an SDK and an MCP server, according to a review written by its competitor folk ([folk: Day AI review](https://www.folk.app/articles/day-ai-review)). Clarify agents are set up from a prompt and "run on a schedule or a signal" ([ChatGate](https://chatgate.ai/post/customer-relationship-agents-by-clarify); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/clarify-6/customer-relationship-agents-by-clarify)).

The incumbents followed. HubSpot announced more than 15 Breeze agents at INBOUND on 3 September 2025 ([BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20250903907062/en/HubSpot-unveils-blueprint-to-building-hybrid-human-AI-teams-with-200-product-updates-at-INBOUND-2025)), and its Data Agent fills "smart properties" on demand, in bulk, from workflows or on a schedule ([HubSpot KB: smart properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/create-smart-properties)) at 10 credits per run ([SpotDev](https://www.spotdev.co.uk/blog/hubspot-breeze-closing-and-data-agents)). Salesforce's Agentforce SDR handles first contact with inbound leads and follows up "with timely nudges if there is no reply" ([Trailhead: Agentforce SDR](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/agentforce-sdr-setup-and-customization/get-to-know-agentforce-sdr)). Pipedrive's Nova, launched on 16 September 2026, prepares calls and proposes updates to stage, value and close date ([LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/pipedrive-launches-nova-ai-assistant-for-crm-updates-52a0540c)). Affinity's Data Update agent proposes field changes "within ~1 minute of note creation" ([Affinity support](https://support.affinity.co/s/article/Review-and-approve-agent-suggestions)).

The AI column is now a standard building block. Attio's AI attributes come in four kinds: summarize, web research, prompt completion and classify. They cannot read notes or emails, and they run only when a user or a workflow triggers them ([Attio: AI attributes](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-ai/ai-attributes)). Clay runs enrichment as a "waterfall": each provider fills only the rows the previous one missed, and its Claygent web agent covers whatever is left ([LeadMagic](https://leadmagic.io/guides/clay-data-enrichment-guide)). The shipped triggers are a call ending (HubSpot runs Smart Deal Progression "after every sales call ends") ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/news/sales-tech/hubspot-spring-2026-smart-deal-progression-aeo-revops)), a note being created (Affinity), new email activity (Lightfield), a thread going quiet (folk) ([folk](https://folk.app)), a record being assigned to the agent (Zoho) ([Zoho community](https://help.zoho.com/portal/id/community/topic/zia-agents-in-zoho-crm-a-better-way-to-set-up-digital-employees)), a schedule, and a manual click. Deduplication is still deterministic rather than agentic.

### AI work appears as a quiet mark that opens onto its evidence

The shipped display patterns are restrained. Attio shows "a small colored dot" next to web-research results (green, yellow or red for confidence), and hovering reveals "the agent's reasoning and the web sources it used". Otherwise AI values sit in ordinary cells "without special badges or icons", and if the AI finds nothing the cell stays blank and no credit is charged ([Attio: AI attributes](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-ai/ai-attributes)). Attio also hides its assistant's reasoning steps by default ([Attio changelog, 29 Apr 2026](https://attio.com/changelog/2026/changelog-april-29-2026)) and shows the credits each chat thread uses ([Attio changelog, 23 Sep 2026](https://attio.com/changelog/2026/changelog-september-23-2026)). HubSpot puts an "AI" button with a Sources tab behind hover on smart properties ([HubSpot KB: smart properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/create-smart-properties)), and its Closing Agent lets reps "hover over a recommendation to see exactly which part of the call it came from" ([SpotDev](https://www.spotdev.co.uk/blog/hubspot-breeze-closing-and-data-agents)). Lightfield shows "the source of the new value in-line as you accept or reject each suggestion" ([Lightfield changelog](https://lightfield.com/blog/suggested-record-updates-support-in-lightfield)).

Affinity's review queue is the most complete design and the one to copy. An **Agent Activity** view groups suggestions by record and by source note. Each row shows the current value with its last-update time next to the proposed value, with per-field checkboxes, "Select all", "Edit", "Reset" and a "View Note" link back to the evidence. Decided items move to a read-only **Activity History**: a green check marks an applied change (with an edit icon if the human altered it), a gray minus marks a declined one, and the log keeps "Suggested Value (edited)" next to what was actually applied ([Affinity support](https://support.affinity.co/s/article/Review-and-approve-agent-suggestions)).

Attribution completes the picture. Clarify links each agent action to "an agent run, which includes the entire reasoning context" ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/clarify-6/customer-relationship-agents-by-clarify)). Zoho gives each Digital Employee its own `@ziaagent.ai` identity, so the audit trail separates agent actions from human ones ([Zoho community](https://help.zoho.com/portal/id/community/topic/zia-agents-in-zoho-crm-a-better-way-to-set-up-digital-employees)). One practitioner sums up the design priority as "The quote matters more than anything else in the design" ([Pravin Kumar](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/should-ai-meeting-notes-write-directly-to-your-crm-2026)). ZoomInfo's guidance says every queued item should show "old value, new value, evidence source, and reason queued" ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/operations/ai-agents-in-crm)).

### Risk tiers decide what an agent writes and what it proposes

Shipped products and practitioner guidance agree on a tiered split:

| Tier | What falls in it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Write automatically, revertible | Notes, call summaries, activity logs, values in agent-owned AI fields, tasks marked as drafts, exact-match dedupe | Granola writes notes only and never changes fields or stages ([Granola](https://www.granola.ai/blog/connect-granola-attio-complete-integration-guide)); Kumar's safe-to-write list ([Kumar](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/should-ai-meeting-notes-write-directly-to-your-crm-2026)) |
| Write, but queue edge cases | Fuzzy dedupe, matching people to companies, scoring | Queue on low confidence, personal email domains, owner conflicts ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/operations/ai-agents-in-crm)) |
| Propose; a human decides | Stage, amount, close date, forecast category, won/lost, owner, records created from transcripts, merges, deletes, any human-entered value | HubSpot Closing Agent: "Reps review, edit, approve or reject each CRM update ... before anything is written" ([SpotDev](https://www.spotdev.co.uk/blog/hubspot-breeze-closing-and-data-agents)); Nova ([LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/pipedrive-launches-nova-ai-assistant-for-crm-updates-52a0540c)); Affinity: "the agent never writes directly" ([Affinity](https://support.affinity.co/s/article/Review-and-approve-agent-suggestions)) |
| Configurable autonomy | Outbound email; any operation once trust is measured | HubSpot Prospecting: "review before sending" or "fully autonomous", starting with review ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/sales/ai-prospecting-agent); [SpotDev](https://www.spotdev.co.uk/blog/hubspot-breeze-prospecting-agent)); Clarify: approve or deny per tool, per agent ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/clarify-6/customer-relationship-agents-by-clarify)) |

Pipedrive's CPTO put the philosophy plainly: "What actually helps a rep isn't more automation. It's more confidence" ([LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/pipedrive-launches-nova-ai-assistant-for-crm-updates-52a0540c)). Affinity routes suggestions to the person who wrote the source note. Its agents only propose changes to fields that person can already edit, and each agent's monitoring is **off by default** ([Affinity support](https://support.affinity.co/s/article/Review-and-approve-agent-suggestions)).

The best evidence for graduating an agent's autonomy is old and from a single case. In a 2024 practitioner account, deal-stage automation ran in suggest mode for a month with **87% acceptance**, then switched to automatic mode with notifications. About 4% of its changes were corrected afterwards, against a 30-40% wrong-stage rate when people updated stages by hand ([Cotera](https://cotera.co/articles/pipedrive-crm-automation-ai)). ZoomInfo's guidance turns this into a rule: track override rates monthly and "promote rarely-overridden actions up a tier" ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/operations/ai-agents-in-crm)).

### External agents enter through MCP servers that inherit the user's permissions

Hosted MCP servers authenticated with OAuth are now standard. HubSpot's became generally available on **13 April 2026**. It uses OAuth 2.1 with PKCE, users "can only access or modify records they already have permission to view or edit", and a `get_user_details` tool reports which tools and objects are available ([HubSpot developer changelog](https://developers.hubspot.com/changelog/remote-hubspot-mcp-server-is-now-generally-available)). Attio's server covers schema discovery, search, get-by-ID, create, **upsert**, update and **merge** of records, list membership, notes, tasks, comments, semantic search over emails, notes and calls, a basic report tool and read-only SQL. It acts with "same permissions as your Attio user account". Its rate limits are tiered by risk:

| Operation type | Limit |
|---|---|
| Reads | 100/s |
| Writes | 25/s |
| Merges | 5/s |
| Search | 300/min |
| Semantic search, reports and SQL | 2/s each |

([Attio MCP docs](https://docs.attio.com/mcp/overview)). Salesforce's hosted MCP servers reached general availability in April 2026 ([Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/mcp-server-for-salesforce/)), and Pipedrive released a public MCP server in June 2026 ([Pipedrive product updates](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/product-updates)).

The safety model has a hole. Attio says write operations "request user confirmation", and one third-party account of HubSpot's server describes a blocking confirmation that names the object, the property, and the current and new values ([tl;dv](https://tldv.io/blog/hubspot-mcp/)). HubSpot's own changelog describes no such mechanism, so the confirmation most likely lives in the client. The research found no vendor MCP server that offers a "propose change" operation as distinct from a direct write. Absence of evidence is not proof, but the pattern is clear: vendors rely on the agent's client to ask permission. CRM System cannot rely on that. CraftBot reads an operations manifest and calls it directly, so the propose-or-write decision has to be enforced by the server for each operation.

### Confidently wrong writes are the failure everyone designs against

The documented failure modes are specific. The first is the plausible but wrong value: "A prospect saying 'we might look at this next quarter' can become a close date" ([Pravin Kumar](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/should-ai-meeting-notes-write-directly-to-your-crm-2026)). The second is overwriting human values: Gong's extractor replaces the previous value "when new or better information is found", with no stated protection for manual edits ([Gong help](https://help.gong.io/docs/ai-data-extractor)). HubSpot shows the reverse lock, where smart properties "cannot be manually overwritten once generated—only removed or regenerated", so a human cannot correct the AI in place ([HubSpot KB: smart properties](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/create-smart-properties)), and its record summary "cannot be manually customized" ([HubSpot KB: summarize records](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/summarize-records)). Enrichment agents that cannot resolve a company fill the gap with invented data, and one enrichment vendor's maxim is that "ten million contacts at 70% accuracy create more damage than five million at 90%" ([Explorium](https://www.explorium.ai/blog/data-for-gtm/ai-agents-traditional-enrichment-failure/)). Users want control: HubSpot customers opened a July 2026 community thread titled "Can you stop Breeze Assistant from making edits in the CRM?" ([HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/tag/breeze/1536)). Costs are unpredictable too, since HubSpot charges credits "even when a value isn't filled" while Attio charges nothing for a blank result ([HubSpot KB](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/properties/create-smart-properties); [Attio](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-ai/ai-attributes)).

The countermeasures in shipping products are evidence quotes, suggest mode before automatic mode, per-field ownership, attribution by actor and run, and a history of suggested versus applied values. The one countermeasure nobody documents shipping is **undo**. ZoomInfo recommends bulk rollback of automated writes ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/operations/ai-agents-in-crm)), but no vendor's agent interface in this research offers it.

## CRM System needs a memory layer before a new coat of paint

CRM System already has most of the surfaces the leaders converged on. The frontend has a command palette, a shortcuts dialog, a dense data table with cell editors, a filter bar, bulk actions, CSV import, a record peek, a kanban board, a record page with Overview, Notes, Tasks, Emails and Files tabs and a timeline panel, plus My Work, Dashboard and Reports pages, an AI chat sheet and an AI summary dialog (`crm-system/frontend/src/app/components`). The weakness is underneath, in two places.

**The agent's interface is thin.** The operations manifest CraftBot reads lists six operations: `health`, `ops.list`, `emails.send`, `ai.chat`, `records.summarize` and `pipeline.summary` (`crm-system/operations.json`). None of them searches, upserts, updates, merges, proposes or undoes anything.

**The schema forgets** (`crm-system/pb/pb_migrations/1700000000_init_crm.js`). A person has one `email`, one `phone` and one `company`. A company has a free-text `domain` with no uniqueness constraint. A deal has a numeric `value` with no currency, no open/won/lost status, no probability and no lost reason, and a stage has only a name, an order and a color. Custom attributes support three types, and `attribute_values` holds one row per attribute and record, so every edit destroys the previous value. Activities store a 500-character body with no actor. Notes, tasks, activities, attachments and emails all cascade-delete with their parent record, and no field anywhere records an actor, a history, a proposal or a deletion time. Every collection also requires a logged-in user, and the app ships login and register pages. That multi-user design is unnecessary weight for a single-user brief, although the agent still needs its own actor identity. The older `LIVING_UI.md` spec, written before the PocketBase port, describes 36px rows, a three-panel record page, merge left out of scope, and reports that sum values across currencies. All four sit below the bar described above.

The redesign should therefore run in two passes, foundations first. The table below sets out what to build, which product to copy, and which documented failure to avoid in each area.

| Area | Must have | Copy from | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memory | Append-only event log stamped with actor (founder, `agent:craftbot`, `import:<id>`, system), run ID, batch ID and a from/to diff; versioned field values | Attio `active_from`/`active_until`/`created_by_actor`; Clarify agent runs; Zoho agent identity | Timeline rows without an actor; changes that never reach the changelog (Pipedrive) |
| Agent review | A `proposals` collection holding current value, proposed value, evidence quote, source link, reason and status; accept, edit or reject one by one or in bulk; history that keeps suggested and applied values | Affinity Agent Activity; Lightfield inline sources; HubSpot Closing Agent | Relying on the agent's client to confirm writes; AI fields humans cannot correct (HubSpot); silent overwrites (Gong) |
| Undo | Batch undo for any agent run, import or bulk edit; soft delete with a 90-day restore and a uniqueness check on restore; reversible merges with ID aliases | HubSpot restore window and email-collision rule; ZoomInfo bulk rollback | Cascading hard deletes; irreversible merges (HubSpot, Pipedrive) |
| Identity | Multi-value, normalized, unique emails and domains; upsert that rejects ambiguous matches and returns the candidates; free-mail blocklist; duplicate review queue; employment history | Attio upsert; HubSpot keys and merge precedence; Salesforce exact-email plus fuzzy-name rule | API writes that skip dedupe (HubSpot companies); auto-merging fuzzy matches |
| Pipelines | Stage probability, won/lost type, rotting days, target days, required fields; stage-event table; lost reasons; currency per deal with conversion frozen at close; many-to-many deal contacts with roles | HubSpot probabilities and stage-time fields; Pipedrive rotting and urgency icons; Attio time-in-stage counter | Adding up amounts in different currencies; live exchange rates after close (Pipedrive); single-contact deals |
| Follow-up | A Today inbox (overdue, today, upcoming, snoozed, done) plus attention rows for rotten deals, deals with no next step, and keep-in-touch reminders that are due; last and next interaction and strength on every row; one-key "log interaction" | Close Inbox; Dex cadence anchored on last interaction; Attio interaction fields; Affinity strength bars | Fixed-date reminders that ignore contact (Folk); slow manual logging (Monica) |
| Shell | Sidebar of objects, lists and favorites; ⌘K, `/`, J/K, `?`, n and t; 13px text and 32px rows; two-pane record page with split scrolling; 500px peek; Focus above History | Attio, Twenty, Close, Pipedrive | Heavy headers; three-panel crowding; empty half-screens; an empty first screen; notes hidden behind the grid (Folk) |
| Reports | Home KPI strip with prior-period deltas and an action list; six preset reports with period and pipeline filters, click-through to records, formula tooltips, CSV export | Pipedrive chart over a table; Attio's five-report recipe; Spekit's chart rules | Report builders; all-time-only charts (folk); undefined win-rate denominators |

The ops API is the part no vendor has finished, so it deserves explicit design. Each operation should declare a default autonomy level that the server enforces, return an event or batch ID, and reject ambiguous matches by returning the candidate IDs:

| Operation family | Example operations | Default autonomy | Precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | `schema.describe`, `whoami`, `ops.list` | Read | Attio attribute definitions and `whoami`; HubSpot `get_user_details` |
| Read | `records.search` (by name, email, domain, URL or note text), `records.get`, `views.query` (filter tree), `timeline.list`, `reports.run`, `events.list` (since a cursor) | Read | Attio MCP search and report tools |
| Capture | `notes.create`, `activities.log`, `tasks.create` (marked as agent-created), `ai_fields.fill` | Automatic, revertible | Granola notes-only writes; Attio AI attributes |
| Upsert | `people.upsert` by email, `companies.upsert` by domain | Automatic when the match is unique | Attio upsert rules |
| Change | `records.update` on human-entered fields, `deals.move_stage`, `deals.close` (with reason), `records.delete` (soft) | Proposal | HubSpot Closing Agent; Affinity; ZoomInfo tier 3 |
| Merge | `records.merge` | Proposal, rate-limited | Attio merge limit; HubSpot precedence |
| Outbound | `emails.send` | Proposal by default; can be switched to automatic | HubSpot Prospecting review mode |
| Review and undo | `proposals.list`, `proposals.decide`, `batches.undo` | The founder decides proposals; the agent may undo its own batches | Affinity history; ZoomInfo rollback |

Being single-user also removes most of what makes incumbent CRMs heavy: permission screens, per-team layouts, shared views, leaderboards and owner routing. That frees effort for keyboard depth and agent safety. "Owner" then distinguishes the founder from the agent rather than one rep from another. Every field gets an ownership flag: `human` (the agent may only propose), `agent` (the agent writes freely, with provenance) or `shared` (the agent may fill the field only while it is empty). This settles the stand-off between HubSpot's locked AI fields and Gong's overwrites.

The UI should keep AI chrome quiet, following Attio. Agent-written values get a small agent mark that opens onto the source, the run and the time. Pending proposals sit in the record's Focus area and in a global review queue, where Y and N accept and reject. A run ledger shows what each agent session read, wrote and proposed, with one undo per session.

## Conclusion

The visual bar for a modern CRM is now a published specification. Twenty's tokens, Attio's record-page anatomy and Close's shortcut map can be matched in weeks. What still separates CRMs in late 2026 lies underneath the screens: whether every change carries an actor, a reason and an inverse. The incumbents attached agents to databases built for human data entry, so they depend on client-side confirmations and still cannot unmerge. A local app designed from the start for two actors can make "the agent proposes, the founder approves, and anything can be undone" a guarantee the server enforces rather than one the client chooses to honor. The underlying insight is that an agent-operated CRM is a multi-user system even with one human in it. Attribution, conflicting edits, audit and permissions all come back, this time between a person and an agent.

Autonomy should be earned per operation, not set once at configuration. The only quantitative evidence available is an older, single-case 87% acceptance rate and ZoomInfo's advice to promote rarely-overridden actions, and both point the same way. CRM System should record acceptance and override rates for each type of proposal, show them next to the review queue, and let the founder promote an operation from proposal to automatic-with-notification once the numbers justify it. Much of the AI evidence in this survey comes from vendors, investors or competitors. No independent study measures how often CRM agents get revenue fields wrong, so the app should be built to produce that evidence about its own agent.
