## How the balance works (decisions)

- A balance is the sum of ledger rows; nothing stores it. Rows a policy implies are PLANNED: grants (`G:`), tenure top-ups (`T:`), monthly accruals (`A:`), hours-worked accruals (`W:`), Japanese base-date grants (`J:`), bank holidays from the allowance (`H:`), the leave year close (`PC:` expire everything, `CO:` carry up to the limit) and lot expiries (`X:`). The engine (`simulate`) works them out from the policy versions and the employment; the daily run and every change (`syncEmployment`) write each key once, and a correcting row (same key, dated on the key's day, note "correction after a change on ...") when a later change moves the target. Reads always run the engine, so a balance is right before the run, and the same walk projects any future day.
- Lots: every credit opens a lot (optionally expiring); leave taken uses the lot that expires first (carry-over before this year's days, the oldest Japanese grant first); what no lot covers is a negative balance the next credit pays back. Leave taken is one ledger row per day (exact as-of balances and FIFO); a cancellation writes reversing rows on the same days.
- Policy edits are versions: `history` [{from, rules}], the version in effect on a day applies; `leave.policy-save --effective_from` (default the start of the current leave year). Changing a policy's leave year start is refused once people have balances on it (make a new policy and assign it from a day). A mid-year policy switch prorates both policies by the 12 slices of the leave year (a slice counts when its 15th day is covered).
- Joining proration: slices covered from the first day (15th-day rule), rounding half, whole or exact; waiting months move the grant day, not the proration start (Singapore: 7 days x 7/12 granted after 3 months).
- Per-hours-worked accrual calls `lib_time.payInputs(...).worked_minutes` per month (the SPEC 3.5 contract of the time module). Reads reuse hours already written; the daily run counts the last two months again (late time corrections). Future months are estimated from the schedule and marked as estimates.
- "Available" = balance on the day minus approved leave after it minus requests waiting. The request check walks the projection with this request and the waiting ones, and refuses when the balance would go below the limit on any day from the start (so leave spanning into the next leave year can use that year's grant).
- Peers see colleagues as away without the type; managers see their team with types; types marked `visibility: away` (Australia family and domestic violence leave) show the type to HR only.

## Void and replace of a pay run (added after payroll-core merged)

- Migration `1730002001_leave_encash_pay.js` adds `reserved_for` and `pay_history` to `leave_encash_requests`.
- `payrun.voided { payrun, replaced_by }`: the cash-ins the void run paid go back to `approved` and unpaid, `reserved_for` the replacement, with a `released` entry in `pay_history` and an audit row. `lib_leave.payInputs` leaves reserved cash-ins out, because payroll's void already copies each one into the replacement's pay inputs (paying it again from `payInputs` would pay it twice).
- `payrun.locked { payrun }`: cash-ins reserved for that run are marked paid when the run carries them (a payslip line or pay input of the run names the cash-in), whether or not payroll also calls `markEncashPaid`.
- The ledger is not reversed: payment writes no ledger rows. The `encash` debit was written at approval, the days stay cashed in across the void, and the replacement pays them; reversing it would give the days back while they are being paid. The release is kept in `pay_history` and the audit trail, nothing is deleted.
- Test `t04_payroll_void.mjs` (21 checks, real payroll module): lock pays it, void releases it, the replacement pays it once with the same amount, `markEncashPaid` again changes nothing, one ledger row, the balance counts the days once.

## QA round (2026-11-05)

- **Lists at 300 and 1,000 people (B-10).** No filter ORs ids any more: policy assignments, ledger rows and waiting requests load by SQL with bound ids in chunks of 500 (`preload`, `assignmentRows`); `roster` reads the people, the job on the day, department, work week and holiday calendar of any number of rows in six queries; `visible` is one query. Every sync (each change to a person's leave, and the daily run) also replaces the person's rows in `leave_balances` (each tracked type's balance view of the day) and `leave_attention` (days that lapse within 61 days, a balance below zero, the five-day rule at risk or missed); `leave.attention`, the daily notices and `leave.balances` read those rows (the balances table adds waiting requests when read and works out live anyone without a row for the day). The daily run syncs 25 employments per transaction. `t05_scale.mjs` at 1,000 people in one entity (median wall time on the build machine, three tries): `leave.home` 0.18 s, `leave.attention` 1.6 s, `leave.balances --limit 500` 2.0 s, `leave.calendar` (month) 1.8 s, `leave.out` (week) 0.4 s, `leave.mine` 0.1 s, the forced daily run with notices 35 s (server time alone: attention 0.5 s, balances 0.6 s, calendar 0.7 s). At 300 people: attention 0.45 s (was 4.4 s), balances 1.7 s, calendar 0.6 s (was 1.5 to 2.8 s), daily run 11 s.
- **Unused leave paid out on termination.** `leave_types.payout_on_exit`, seeded on where the law requires it (SG, MY, AU and GB annual leave, IN earned leave, US PTO for a California entity; off in Japan), editable in Settings, Types (and `leave.types-save --payout_on_exit`). `separation.approved` and `separation.changed` (lifecycle) run `lib_leave_req.onSeparation`: per such type, an approved cash-in of kind `exit` for the balance on the last day (grants and accruals cut to that day), `pay_on` the last day, with its ledger row; payroll pays it through the existing `payInputs().encash` in the final pay run (tested end to end with the real lifecycle and payroll). While unpaid it follows every change (a moved last day moves it, leave taken before the last day lowers it, at zero it is withdrawn; ledger rows are moved or corrected, never deleted). A paid one never changes: more balance after a later last day is a second cash-in, less is written to the audit trail for payroll. Hours types are paid as hours divided by the person's hours a day.
- **Accrual.** `accrual_day: daily` (progressive; the default for Australian annual and personal/carer's leave): a finished month is one ledger row, the month in progress accrues day by day in the walk and is written when it ends, so a new hire can take accrued leave in the first month. A leaver's balance counts only to the last day: monthly accrual prorates the last month by days; fixed and tenure grants with proration `join` prorate the leaving year (whole months by their 15th day, the last month by its days).
- **`expiring`** (balance contract, `leave.mine`, `leave.balance`, the table dot, attention, notices) lists what is lost if nothing more is taken: lot expiries and, at the next year close, the part over the carry-over limit (types that carry nothing over, like sick leave, start each year afresh and are not counted), merged by day: `{ day: the last day to use them, expires: the day they are gone, amount }`; `leave.balance` lots carry `use_by` next to `expires`. (CLI test job 2: 10 days lapse at the year close, not 5.)
- **Server texts carry days, not formatted dates.** Approval summaries read "Annual leave, 3 days (Sam Wong)" (the days are in the approval's fields); the profile facts are the kernel's typed `ProfileFact`: balances `{ kind: 'days' | 'hours', value: 12.5 }`, on leave until and the next leave `{ kind: 'date', value: '2026-11-16' }`, requests waiting `{ kind: 'count' }`; the kernel formats them.
- **UI (UI QA L1 to L7).** Request time off opens on the calendar with the person's usual type (`leave.mine` marks it `usual`) as a chip above it: Request, the day, Send; no instruction text. Type marks are ink icons on a hairline square (colour stays on the wallchart bars and their legend). The employee balances are one panel of hairline rows. Leave home numbers its tiles 01.1 to 01.4 and its panels 02 to 05; the attention panel shows a skeleton while it reads. The Home widgets number their panels with the host's `index` and use a default button (Home owns the one primary); My leave wraps type names and shows what is waiting. Settings drops the code column and widens "Counted in". Balances follows the sidebar entity (all entities: one table per entity). ## Known gaps

- US state paid sick leave: only California is seeded (the brief's example); `data/countries/us.js` has no state minimums. If packs-west adds `leave_types` rows with a `region` (NY, WA, NJ, CO, IL, MA, AZ, ...), seeding picks them up with no code change.
- Japan: the 80 percent attendance condition is assumed met (HR reverses a grant with an adjustment); a company-wide base date (斉一的取扱い) and early grants are not modelled (`first_after_months` sets the first grant only). Hourly leave is capped by `max_hours_per_year`.
- Malaysia and Singapore tenure steps apply from the leave year start (or the anniversary for the month-based sick ladders), not prorated within the year.
- No "maximum absent per department" rule (the clash list is a warning only).
- Leave pay amounts are payroll's: `payInputs` gives days.
- `leave_balances` and `leave_attention` are as of the last sync: between local midnight and the daily run (00:30) they hold yesterday's rows (the balances table works those people out live). Bank holiday edits in the kernel calendars reach them at the next run.
- Lifecycle's settlement statement lists the balances of `encashable` types; with payout on termination it should list the types with `payout_on_exit` (or the person's exit cash-ins, `leave.encashments`). That is lifecycle's file.
- The register prints with the browser's print of the page (no dedicated print route).
