## Contracts (exact)

- `lib_time.payInputs(app, employmentId, from, to)` -> exactly the SPEC 3.5 keys: `{ worked_minutes, regular_minutes, overtime: [{ component, minutes, multiplier }], absent_days, late_minutes, night_minutes, holiday_worked_minutes, issues: [{ code, message, severity }] }`.
  - `overtime` lists every rule bucket of the period: overtime components (multiplier is the whole share of the hourly rate: 1.5 = time and a half, the minutes are not in `regular_minutes`) and the night premium (`NIGHT25`, multiplier 0.25, paid on top: its minutes are also in regular or overtime). Payroll pays `minutes / 60 x hourly rate x multiplier` per component.
  - Component codes the defaults use: `OT125`, `OT135`, `OT150`, `OT200`, `OT300`, `RD200`, `PH200`, `PH250`, `SUN200`, `NIGHT25`. Payroll needs pay components with these codes (or the entity edits the codes in Time settings).
  - `adjustments` (2026-11-05, UI round): `[{ day, component, multiplier, minutes, days?, reason, locked_by_payrun }]`, the difference on days an earlier locked run used that changed afterwards (a punch is never refused, so a late clock out on a paid day lands here). Components: `regular` (multiplier 1), the overtime and premium codes with their multiplier, `late` (minutes), `absent` (minutes 0, `days` the change in absent days). The week and month around a changed day are classified as paid and as now; a row on a day that did not change itself is overtime moving inside that week or month (reason says so). Days of an approved timesheet keep its hours. Pay them in this run; `lockDays` then marks them paid.
  - Issue messages carry no written dates: the days travel as `days`, weeks as `weeks`, the month of an overtime limit as `month` (format them with the screen's date helpers).
  - Issue codes: `adjustments_after_lock` (warning: adjustments to pay), `missing_punch` (entity setting, warning by default), `attendance_requests_pending` (warning), `timesheet_unapproved` and `timesheet_missing` (blocker when the entity pays from required timesheets), `overtime_not_approved` (warning; worked overtime is still paid), `days_locked` (warning: another locked run used some days), `period_open` (warning: the period runs past today), `ot_month_over_warn`, `ot_year_over_warn`, `ot_months_over_warn` (always warnings), `ot_month_over_limit`, `ot_average_over_limit`, `ot_year_over_limit` (entity setting, warning by default).
  - Days without a saved attendance row are computed on the fly (nothing written). An approved timesheet's hours replace the punches for its days.
- `lib_time.lockDays(app, employmentId, from, to, payrunId) -> { locked, created, skipped_future, settled }` (for payroll at lock, inside its transaction): every day up to today gets a saved attendance row and is locked to the run; approved timesheets inside the period lock too; `settled` counts the open adjustments (days up to `to`) this run now pays. A locked day keeps what was paid. Facts are never refused: punches (clock, kiosk, corrections) and approved attendance requests (missed punch, correction, work from home, on duty) and shift requests on a locked day are recorded; the row keeps what was paid, notes what the day is now (`after_lock` with `source` auto or request, `adjusted_after_lock`) and the difference is an adjustment for the next run. Once a request decided a locked day, later punches do not change it; voiding the run opens the day with the request's values. Only a hand edit of a locked day's figures (attendance.mark, mark-bulk, reset) is refused, 409 `day_locked`, with a message to file an attendance request instead. Payroll should lock only days up to the run's cutoff (never later than today).
- `lib_time.unlockDays(app, payrunId) -> { unlocked, reopened }` (for payroll when it voids a locked run before payment): its days open again and follow their punches; adjustments it paid are open again (`reopened`).
- `lib_time.payInputsDetail(...)` = the same plus `detail` (per day), used by the op `time.pay-inputs`.
- `lib_time.payInputsMany(app, employmentIds, from, to)` -> `{ [employmentId]: <payInputs result> }` and `lib_time.payInputsDetailMany(app, employmentIds, from, to)` -> `{ [employmentId]: <payInputsDetail result> }` (2026-11-04, speed round): the same answers as the single calls, read together (one query per kind of data for up to 100 people at a time). Payroll should call `payInputsMany` once per run, reports `payInputsDetailMany` once per report. An unknown id fails the whole call with 404, as the single call would; duplicate ids are answered once.
- Speed (scratch copy of APP's merged state, demo seed, a machine at full CPU from other builders, best of 5): per person-month the old payInputs took 64 to 250 ms; now `payInputs` takes 6 to 13 ms and `payInputsMany` 4 to 10 ms (300 people about 1.5 to 3 s). `payInputsDetailMany` is within a millisecond of `payInputsMany`. `bench.mjs` proves the answers equal the old code's (detail included) for every employment of the seed over 10 periods, with and without saved attendance.
- `lib_time.profileFacts(ctx, employmentId)` -> `[{ label, value, tone, link }]` (today's shift, clock state, this month).

## Decisions

- Punches are events: a correction adds a punch or voids one with a reason; nothing edits a punch. Sessions run from an in to the next out; a session belongs to the day of its in (night shifts work). An in with no out after 16 hours is a missing clock out.
- Only what was published is the plan (drafts change nothing for people or attendance). Publishing re-works past days whose plan changed.
- Default counting is `exception` (no clock needed; a working day counts as worked) so payroll never deducts for a company that does not use the clock; an entity switches to `punch` in Time settings. A rule set per employment type (`applies`) covers hourly versus salaried staff.
- The scheduled break is taken off a day over 6 hours (setting) when no break was punched.
- Unapproved overtime under pre-approval is still paid (worked time is owed) and flagged.
- The kiosk device token travels in the `X-Kiosk-Device` header (never a query string). Kiosk PINs are 4 to 6 digits, bcrypt, locked 15 minutes after 5 wrong tries.
- The rule engine is pure (`lib_time_rules.js`), tested in Node; daily tiers, weekly on what is left, monthly re-rating of overtime, seventh day, rest day by weekday, holiday tiers, night premium; caps follow the 36 agreement (warn values exclude rest-day work, the 100 hour and average limits include it).
- Pay inputs read in bulk (`resolver(app).preload(ids, from, to)`): employments and job records, then over the union of everyone's windows the shift assignments, approved leave, saved attendance (as one JSON text per query, so few crossings into the script runtime), the history the overtime limits look back on (only rows with a status), timesheets with their approved hours and pending attendance requests; punches and approved overtime requests on first need. Every reader falls back to its own query when a range is not preloaded, so other callers of the resolver behave as before. The limits' history keeps only days with worked or leave minutes (any other day adds nothing to a bucket) plus the list of months it spans.
- Country defaults are editable starting points with notes, not legal advice (Singapore rest-day and holiday pay is by the day and approximated as 2x; Australia is award placeholders).

- UI round 2026-11-05 (QA's time findings): T8 punches are never refused (see lockDays, adjustments); T1 the clock shows the new state at once (optimistic clock status), the location and the server follow, a second tap waits its turn, and the toast offers Undo (`clock.undo`: your own latest punch within five minutes); T2 without a clock the board follows the time of day (before the shift Expected later, after it "Shift over" via `shift_over`, the Done tile counts them); T4 time settings: tabs below the header, the country notes as one line plus a hint, each rule's note as a hint, no money written in the notes; T5 a member without an employee record gets quiet empty states (no request for their own clock, schedule, timesheet or kiosk PIN; `kiosk.pin-status` answers `no_employee`); T7 the type scale (10.5 mono labels, 12, 13, 14, 15, 26) in every time page; day cells and shift chips are 40 px tall on touch screens; dates in time pages go through `format.ts` (also aria labels). Kernel parts still under 40 px on phones (Button, Input, Segmented, Tabs, Toggle, Hint) are kernel-frontend's K6.
- Facts (2026-11-05): time approvals (attendance_request, overtime, timesheet, shift_request) and notices (shifts changed, swap asked, answered and approved) carry structured `facts` (days or ranges, hours as `number` with unit h, the new shift, states); the plain summaries and titles stay for the CLI. `lib_time.fact` builds them. The time module has no Home widgets (the clock and team cards on Home are home-reports'). Op descriptions are at most 200 characters; their shapes and detail are guide rules (`app.guide` time).
- `timesheets.review-ask` (fix round 2026-11-04): time editors ask for an entity or every entity they cover (a role limited to several entities names one); a manager without time edit, or anyone with `--team`, asks for their own team only (job `ref_collection` employees, `payload.people` lists the employment ids, `time.review --people` keeps your AI agent to them); everyone else gets 403. The Timesheets page shows the button only to time editors and managers.

## Known gaps

- Overtime slip (Frappe) is the approved timesheet plus `payInputs`; there is no separate slip document.
- No auto-approval of clean timesheets (approval flows auto-approve by amount or days only).
- No offline punch queue on the kiosk; no face match (by design: PIN only).
- Large companies: the board reads in bulk (2026-11-04: 300 people 1.0 to 1.8 s server time before, 0.25 to 0.45 s after on a fully loaded machine; `scale_seed.mjs` builds the 300-person company, `board_check.mjs` proves the answers unchanged). The week grid and the month grid still read per person (about 25 ms a person). `roster` reads its pool with `activePool`, the same rule as `lib_people.activeEmployments` in three queries (the kernel's version looks up two records a person): if the kernel changes who counts as active, change `activePool` too.
- Vehicle logs are the expenses module's (mileage).
