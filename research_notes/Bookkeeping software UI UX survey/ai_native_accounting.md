# AI-native and automation-first accounting: how an AI agent participates in bookkeeping (2025-2026)

Scope: Digits, Puzzle, Pilot, Bench (shutdown), Rillet, Campfire, Kick, Booke.ai, Docyt, Truewind, Basis, Ramp, Brex, plus QuickBooks (Intuit) agents and Xero JAX. Research date: 2026-10-28.

Source-reliability legend used throughout:
- **[Vendor]**: company website, help center, blog or press release. These are marketing claims unless they describe concrete UI behavior from docs.
- **[Docs]**: vendor help-center or product documentation that describes UI behavior. More reliable than marketing pages for "how it works".
- **[Press]**: trade press (CPA Practice Advisor, Insightful Accountant, SiliconANGLE, TechCrunch, GeekWire). Usually re-reports vendor statements.
- **[3P]**: third-party blogs, aggregators and review sites. Several of these are competitors or SEO content (for example usecarly.com, beancount.io, finlens, dupple), so treat them as low to medium reliability.
- A note on beancount.io: it is a plain-text-accounting vendor. Its blog posts and forum threads (users like "bookkeeper_bob", "accountant_alice") look like synthetic or editorial personas. I quote them only as an illustration of practitioner arguments, not as verified user testimony.

## 1. Which tasks each product automates end to end

### Takeaway
By 2026 every product in this set automates categorization (coding) and bank/card matching, and most claim continuous reconciliation. The newer ERP-class products (Rillet, Campfire, Puzzle, Digits) have moved on to close work: accruals, prepaid and fixed-asset schedules, flux/variance commentary, anomaly and duplicate scans, and close checklists. Only Pilot claims fully autonomous bookkeeping with "zero human intervention", and it publishes no accuracy metric. Everyone else describes "management by exception": the AI posts what it is confident about and routes the rest to a human.

### Cited Findings

**Digits (AI-native GL, "Autonomous General Ledger", AGL)**
- Launched "Digits Accounting Agents" on June 23, 2025. They run whole workflows (bookkeeping, dashboards/KPIs, management reporting) and pause "only when human judgment is needed or desired". Digits says the AGL had powered 2,000+ month-end closes and that 700+ firms had applied to its partner program. [Vendor/Press] — [GlobeNewswire press release via search snippet](https://www.globenewswire.com/fr/news-release/2025/06/23/3103524/0/en/Digits-Launches-First-AI-Agents-for-Accounting-Workflows-Built-on-Digits-Autonomous-General-Ledger.html); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2025/digits-adds-ai-agents-accounting-platform/)

[Vendor] — [Digits Agentic Close](https://digits.com/product/agentic-close/)
- Reconciliation in the docs: Digits matches statement lines to ledger transactions, drafts the reconciliation and surfaces exceptions (missing, duplicate or unmatched items). Status updates continuously as items are resolved. Document extraction covers statements, bills and receipts. [Docs] — [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai)

[Docs] — [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai)

### Inferences
- Across products, the automation stack is layered in the same order: (1) ingest and normalize, (2) categorize with party/vendor identification, (3) match to bank statement lines and documents, (4) schedule-driven entries (accruals, prepaids, depreciation, revenue recognition), (5) a continuous QC scan (duplicates, anomalies, miscoding), (6) close-time flux commentary and reports. A local app can model its agent's job list on Digits' six stages (Collect, Book, Reconcile, Schedule, Review, Report).
- Collections and invoice chasing are the least developed agent task in this set. Only QuickBooks (Payments Agent) and Rillet (an AR-reminder flow) describe it concretely. That leaves room for a simple, well-designed version: a draft reminder email per overdue invoice for the user to review.
- "End to end" means two different things. Pilot means no human at all, with an audit history available afterwards. Digits, Campfire, Rillet and Puzzle mean the agent does everything but pauses at confidence or materiality gates. The second model is the defensible one for a single-user app.

- I found no independent detail on how Ramp's receipt matching works internally. The launch blog emphasizes coding, accruals and reconciliation.

## 2. Review UX: confidence, review queues, explanations, evidence, bulk/keyboard review, learning from corrections, auto-post vs propose

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is a **confidence-gated split**. High-confidence items post automatically (Digits, Xero JAX, Ramp, optionally Campfire). Low-confidence items go to a review queue, which Digits frames as a "training queue, not permanent workload". The best implementations show *why* (rule, document match, memory of past user decisions, or global prediction) and *evidence* (influential past transactions, source documents). Corrections teach the system immediately, and Ramp lets users correct in plain language, which becomes a standing rule. Incumbents (QuickBooks) take the stricter stance that nothing posts without approval.

### Cited Findings

**Confidence gating and auto-post vs propose**
- Digits proposes a category and a party (vendor/customer) for each incoming transaction, assigns a confidence score, and either posts automatically (high confidence) or escalates to the "Checklist → Book" queue. It is "intentionally conservative when certainty is low". [Docs] — [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai)
- Digits' published auto-post rates vary by source and date: "auto-books 93%" (search snippet), "95%+ of transactions post without human intervention" (product page), and "96.2% Zero-Touch Transactions™" (help center). [Vendor] — [Digits Agentic Close](https://digits.com/product/agentic-close/); [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai)
- Xero JAX "only automatically reconciles a transaction if there's high confidence". The feature is **off by default** and must be turned on, and users can "take over the reconciliation at any point". [Press] — [Insightful Accountant](https://blog.insightfulaccountant.com/xero-rolling-out-jax-powered-automatic-bank-reconciliation); [eCommerceNews](https://ecommercenews.com.au/story/xero-rolls-out-ai-bank-reconciliation-with-jax-tool)
- Ramp: low-risk routine items auto-sync (its example is "$17 Uber ride"). When judgment is needed, the agent "shares the context", checking "submission completeness, coding certainty, and vendor history". Users set agent permissions per capability: **code, review, sync**, or combinations. [Vendor] — [Ramp blog](https://ramp.com/blog/accounting-agent-launch)
- Campfire Ember: "built-in AI confidence thresholds" let teams set "exactly what auto-applies versus what requires approval", adjustable "as comfort grows". The same pages also say "all agent actions require review before posting", which is internally inconsistent. The most likely reading is that review is the default and auto-apply is opt-in by threshold. [Vendor] — [Campfire Ember](https://campfire.ai/ember); [Campfire blog](https://campfire.ai/blog/introducing-ember-agents)
- QuickBooks: agents "never post transactions or take action without your approval". The bank feed "pre-select[s] transactions ready for posting" and users approve them. [Vendor] — [QuickBooks Business Feed](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/sg/r/product-update/quickbooks-business-feed/); [Unofficial QuickBooks Accountants Podcast (July 2025)](https://unofficialquickbooksaccountantspodcast.transistor.fm/101)
- Puzzle states "Nothing posts to the GL without your explicit sign-off", with a review panel showing "every proposed entry, every reasoning step, every source transaction". The page lives under a "/wip/" path. Puzzle's blog describes the model as "drafted by software and AI, and reviewed and improved by accountants. No self driving accounting AI or changes made autonomously behind your back." [Vendor] — [Puzzle AI (WIP)](https://puzzle.io/wip/features/puzzle-ai); [Puzzle blog](https://puzzle.io/blog/puzzle-accounting-ai). This sits awkwardly with Puzzle's own claim that it auto-categorizes 85-95% of transactions. Categorization may be applied while close entries need sign-off. I could not verify which.
- Rillet: "Nothing auto-posts without human approval" (third-party summary). The accruals module offers "editable suggestions, flexible approve/post workflows, and overrides". [3P/Vendor] — [SoftwareConnect via search snippet](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/rillet/); [Rillet January 2026 update](https://www.rillet.com/blog/january-2026-product-update)

**How corrections teach the system**
- Digits "Instant Learning": each confirmation or correction "applies immediately to similar future transactions", learning at client, firm and global level. Connected-app mappings (Stripe, BILL, Gusto) are a separate deterministic layer. Changing one mapped transaction changes only that transaction, and future mapped transactions keep following the mapping. [Docs] — [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai)
- Ramp: users correct "in plain language, like 'Code Uber rides for the LA sales team to Client travel going forward'". The system "learns from the change and the explanation". Ramp claims a 70% reduction in corrections within the first month and 98% accuracy on "ready to sync" decisions. [Vendor] — [Ramp blog](https://ramp.com/blog/accounting-agent-launch)
- Kick: suggested rules that users adapt (conditions on vendor and transaction type). Flagging a personal-vs-business transaction creates a rule for similar future items. [Vendor] — [kick.co](https://kick.co)
- Booke learns from "each approval and correction" and from up to two years of history, but only per client (an "isolated AI Brain", with no cross-client sharing). [Vendor] — [Booke for Xero](https://booke.ai/xero); [Booke](https://booke.ai/uk)
- Rillet: users write "deterministic rules based on merchant names or transaction descriptions" for bank rec and mapping, and can give Aura "additional context to instantly refine the analysis". [Vendor] — [Rillet January 2026 update](https://www.rillet.com/blog/january-2026-product-update)
- Puzzle (WIP) describes a four-step lifecycle for AI-authored close logic. **Describe**: plain-language instructions. **Lock**: "logic becomes deterministic". **Approve**: review panel plus sign-off. **Review & Finalize**: an accuracy scan. Its principle is "Same inputs, same outputs. Every run." [Vendor] — [Puzzle AI (WIP)](https://puzzle.io/wip/features/puzzle-ai)
- Rillet Aura Flow (a natural-language workflow builder) shows "every step before running" and logs "every field, dimension, and decision". [Vendor] — [Rillet](https://www.rillet.com/blog/mcp-aura-flow-and-aura-agents-which-one-for-which-job)

**Client and owner questions as part of review**
- Puzzle routes questions to the client by email and accepts natural-language replies [Vendor] — [Puzzle blog](https://puzzle.io/blog/puzzle-accounting-ai). QuickBooks lets accountants "initiate chats with your clients right in QuickBooks" (beta), and the agent checks the reply before posting [Vendor] — [QuickBooks AU](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/au/blog/accountants-and-bookkeepers/quickbooks-intuit-ai-agents-for-accountants/). Booke has one-click client queries [Vendor] — [Booke](https://booke.ai/xero).

### Inferences
- Transparent apps use three provenance tiers: **Deterministic rule → Matched document → Learned from your past decisions → Model prediction**. Xero's four Methods map onto them directly. A local app should store a method enum on every agent-created posting and show it as a column or badge.
- Corrections should become **visible, editable rules**, not silent model updates. Puzzle's Lock step, Kick's suggested rules, Ramp's plain-language standing instructions and Rillet's deterministic merchant rules all point this way. Digits instead rejects fixed if-this-then-that rules in favor of learning. For a single-user local app with an LLM agent, explicit rules are easier to audit and replay.
- Permission per capability (Ramp's code/review/sync) is a good model for an agent that operates through the same operations as the UI. Grant the agent "propose" by default and let the user raise specific operations to "post automatically under threshold X".
- I found no evidence of keyboard-first review in any product. Inline editing (QBO), bulk changes (Digits) and a guarded Confirm button (Digits) are the documented affordances.

### Gaps
- I found no product documentation showing numeric confidence values to users (for example "87%"). Most products appear to show only a bucket (auto vs needs review) plus reasons. Puzzle's blog mentions "confidence scoring with explanations" without UI detail.
- No product documents keyboard shortcuts for review queues in the sources I could access.
- I could not determine how Digits exposes "global learning" provenance to users (whether a suggestion is marked as coming from cross-customer patterns), compared with Xero's explicit "Prediction".

## 3. Audit trail of AI actions: who did what, reversibility, versioning, "AI-generated" markers

### Takeaway
Every vendor claims a "full audit trail", but the concrete patterns are few and good. There are activity logs per transaction (Digits), method labels on auto-actions with remove-and-redo (Xero), side-by-side edit diffs with AI plain-English summaries (Rillet), logs of "date, source, reasoning, and who signed off" (Puzzle WIP), and snapshotting of the data an AI answer used (Rillet, third-party claim). Reversibility appears as undo/redo on the reconciliation or edit/reverse/reclassify on the transaction.

### Cited Findings

[Vendor] — [Pilot blog](https://pilot.com/blog/pilot-unveils-ai-accountant-a-major-leap-toward-artificial-general-intelligence-in-accounting)

### Inferences
- A minimal credible audit model for a local agent-operated ledger has four parts:
  1. An actor on every mutation: user, agent, rule or import.
  2. A method and confidence on agent actions.
  3. A reason string plus evidence references (source document, matched statement line, prior transactions, rule id).
  4. Before/after diffs with a plain-English summary.
- Corrections to posted entries should be reversing entries or reclassifications rather than in-place edits, so history survives. This is standard double-entry practice. No vendor here publicly advertises it, but Digits' "reverse" and Ramp's and Campfire's reversal entries point the same way.
- Rillet's (unverified) idea of snapshotting the data an AI answer used is especially relevant to "explain this number". When the agent explains a figure, store the query or filters and the ledger version or hash it read, so the explanation can be re-checked after later edits.

### Gaps
- I found no public documentation of an explicit "AI-generated" badge on journal lines in Digits, Puzzle or Campfire. Xero's Method column is the only concrete, documented marker.
- I found no vendor documentation of period locking or ledger versioning tied to AI actions. Standard period-close locks presumably exist but were not surfaced in these sources.

## 6. Data model ideas: what AI-native GLs changed vs QuickBooks

### Takeaway
Public detail is thin, and most of it is vendor positioning. Several ideas recur. (a) The ledger was built from scratch to be operated by agents (Digits, Puzzle). (b) An **event-based** ledger maintains **cash and accrual bases simultaneously** (Puzzle). (c) The GL is **perpetual/continuous** rather than period-batched (Rillet). (d) **Multi-entity hierarchies** with reporting at any node and intercompany automation (Rillet, Campfire, Kick). (e) Rich **dimensions** coded per transaction: department, class, location, custom (Ramp, Campfire). (f) **Party (vendor/customer) as a first-class field** on every transaction (Digits). (g) **Deterministic connector mappings** kept separate from learned categorization (Digits). I found no public source documenting an immutable or event-sourced ledger with replay in these products, beyond Puzzle's "event-based" wording.

### Cited Findings
- Puzzle: a "modern, event-based ledger" where "dual-basis accounting means maintaining both cash-basis and accrual-basis books simultaneously", "always in sync without extra work". [Vendor] — [Puzzle blog](https://puzzle.io/blog/puzzle-accounting-ai). The ledger was "built from scratch as an AI-native ledger rather than AI bolted onto legacy software". [3P] — [SoftwareConnect: Puzzle](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/puzzle-accounting-software/)
- Digits: "designed from day one to work with AI agents", using "custom-trained ML models" rather than general-purpose LLMs. CEO Jeff Seibert: "Automating accounting with high accuracy requires building an AI-native system from the ground up." [Press] — [Insightful Accountant](https://blog.insightfulaccountant.com/digits-launches-ai-agents-for-its-autonomous-general-ledger)
- Digits proposes "a category and party (vendor/customer)" per transaction, so party is part of the categorization output. Connected-app mappings route transaction types to fixed accounts independent of learning. Document routing depends on upload location: "Import Bank Transactions" creates ledger transactions, while "Vault" stores documents only. [Docs] — [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai)
- Rillet: a tree-structured subsidiary hierarchy, with reports run at the top level, any parent node or an individual subsidiary. Multi-entity consolidation and GAAP reporting. [3P] — [Rillet search summary (SoftwareConnect/Sacra)](https://sacra.com/c/rillet/). Aura Flow saves "your chart of accounts and dimensions as context". [Vendor] — [Rillet](https://www.rillet.com/blog/mcp-aura-flow-and-aura-agents-which-one-for-which-job)
- Revenue recognition as a native module: Rillet links contract metadata → invoices → payments → ASC 606 schedules and JEs [3P] — [SoftwareConnect: Rillet](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/rillet/). Kick has "Revenue Recognition for live earned revenue tracking" [Vendor] — [kick.co](https://kick.co).
- Campfire: multi-entity, "custom dimensions beyond standard accounts", intercompany reconciliation and accrual subledgers [Vendor] — [Campfire Ember](https://campfire.ai/ember). It positions itself as a NetSuite replacement [3P] — [ERP Research](https://erpresearch.com/erp/campfire).
- Ramp codes "GL, department, class, location, and any custom field", plus item-level coding on invoices. [Vendor] — [Ramp blog](https://ramp.com/blog/accounting-agent-launch)
- Kick: "Multi-entity journal entries that balance automatically" and automatic intercompany transfer accounting. [Vendor] — [kick.co](https://kick.co)
- Bench's ledger was proprietary rather than QuickBooks or Xero, which made exports and switching providers hard when it shut down (see section 7). [3P] — [Sacra: Bench data portability](https://sacra.com/chat/h/328599ff-1b23-4021-9402-12ef274256de/); [Wiss](https://wiss.com/bench-accounting-shutdown-then-buyout-exposes-ai-bookkeeping-challenges/)

### Inferences
- Ideas a local double-entry app can adopt cheaply:
  - Store journal lines with a `basis` capability so cash and accrual views derive from one store (Puzzle's dual-basis idea).
  - Make **party** and **dimensions** first-class columns.
  - Keep **mappings/rules** (deterministic) separate from **learned suggestions** (probabilistic).
  - Separate a **document vault** from the **transaction import** path (Digits).
  - Model scheduled items (prepaids, depreciation, deferred revenue, accruals with auto-reversal) as schedule objects that generate entries, rather than as ad-hoc JEs.
- An append-only journal (corrections via reversing entries) plus an activity log of proposals and decisions gives most of the "event-sourced" benefit without a full event-sourcing architecture. This is my inference; no vendor documented its internals.

### Gaps
- No vendor in the set publicly documents immutability, event sourcing or ledger versioning in technical terms. Puzzle's "event-based" is the only explicit claim, and it is undocumented.
- Digits' internal ledger model (for example, how "parties" and real-time statements are computed) is not described in sources I could access.

## 7. Failure modes and criticism

### Takeaway
The main risks are: (1) **systematic** miscategorization that spot checks miss (one vendor coded wrong every time); (2) liability, since "the AI did it" is no defense for accountants; (3) fragile bank feeds undermining "autonomous" claims; (4) explainability gaps; (5) unverifiable vendor accuracy claims (vendor-run benchmarks, no metrics from Pilot or Docyt); (6) forced rollouts and tier-gating at incumbents (QuickBooks, no opt-out); and (7) the **Bench lesson**: a proprietary, non-portable ledger plus an unsustainable services cost base led to an overnight lockout of thousands of businesses.

### Cited Findings

**Bench (shutdown and aftermath)**
- On December 27, 2024, Bench emailed customers that the platform was immediately inaccessible. It had raised over $100M, including a $60M Series C in 2021. Its model was cloud software plus in-house human bookkeepers. [3P] — [Wikipedia: Bench Accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_Accounting); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/27/bench-shuts-down-leaving-thousands-of-businesses-without-access-to-accounting-and-tax-docs)
- Reported scale varies by source. Customers: "over 11,000 businesses" (search summary from GeekWire/others), "12,000 small businesses" (Dare Obasanjo, quoted), and "over 35,000 U.S. customers" (quoted in TechCrunch coverage, possibly cumulative). Employees: "450" (search summary) vs "approximately 650" (Wikipedia). [3P] — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/vancouver-fintech-company-bench-accounting-announces-sudden-shutdown/); [mjtsai.com roundup](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/01/02/bench-shut-down-then-acquired); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_Accounting)
- Customers were told to file IRS extensions, had until March 2025 to download data, and got no refunds. Pilot offered free migrations from Bench to QuickBooks Online, even for non-Pilot customers. [3P] — [mjtsai.com roundup](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/01/02/bench-shut-down-then-acquired)

Bench filed for bankruptcy in Canada on January 7, 2025, with about $65.4M in liabilities and $2.8M in cash. [3P] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bench_Accounting); [Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/business/20798-acquisition-saves-bench-accounting-from-abrupt-closure)

[3P] — [search summary of LinkedIn post via ecommercefastlane/TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/27/bench-shuts-down-leaving-thousands-of-businesses-without-access-to-accounting-and-tax-docs)

**Practitioner skepticism (low-to-medium reliability sources)**
- Arguments raised: professional liability ("the AI made a mistake" is not a defense with the IRS or state boards), so accountants still review 100% and time shifts from entry to verification. Proposed controls include **balance assertions** to catch drift, rule-based handling for recurring vendors with ML for the rest and humans for tax-critical items, and moving to sampling after a transaction type has been verified about 100 times. [3P; possibly synthetic forum personas] — [beancount.io forum](https://beancount.io/forum/t/the-80-20-reality-ai-categorizes-80-but-i-still-review-100-of-transactions/3217); [beancount.io forum: "98% accuracy but 2% errors compound"](https://beancount.io/forum/t/ai-bookkeeping-tools-promise-98-accuracy-but-the-2-errors-compound-into-tax-penalties-how-do-you-validate-ai-categorization-at-scale/4031)
- Other criticisms of Digits: an "explainability gap" (accountants want to know *why*), fear of "silent, compounding errors", Plaid connections that "suddenly fail" and need re-authentication (fragile for an "autonomous" system), and weak export discoverability. Digits itself acknowledges that complex accruals need manual intervention. [3P] — [beancount.io blog](https://beancount.io/blog/2025/08/05/digits-ai-accountant-balancing-brilliant-dashboards-with-the-need-for-human-trust)
- Cross-client learning is a design choice with privacy implications. Digits learns at client, firm and **global** level [Docs] — [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai). Xero's "Prediction" method uses "similar transactions by all Xero users" [Press] — [Insightful Accountant](https://blog.insightfulaccountant.com/xero-rolling-out-jax-powered-automatic-bank-reconciliation). Booke markets the opposite: an isolated per-client brain with "no cross-client sharing, ever" [Vendor] — [Booke](https://booke.ai/uk).
- Hallucination risk is acknowledged implicitly. Xero markets "JAX Assure" to reduce hallucinations [Vendor] — [Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/xeros-ai-financial-superagent-jax-launches-powerful-new-features/). Puzzle (WIP) contrasts "deterministic" locked logic ("Same inputs, same outputs. Every run") with probabilistic AI [Vendor] — [Puzzle AI (WIP)](https://puzzle.io/wip/features/puzzle-ai). Digits refuses tax and legal questions [Docs] — [How AI Works in Digits](https://help.digits.com/firms-agentic-general-ledger/digits-ai).

### Inferences
- Bench's lesson for a local single-user app is a feature, not a risk: the user owns the data locally. The app should still make standard exports (CSV journal, trial balance, possibly QBO/Xero import formats) first-class and visible, because export discoverability was a criticism even of Digits.
- Systematic-error risk argues for review UX that groups by **vendor/rule** (approve or correct all lines from vendor X at once, then convert the result into a rule) rather than only line-by-line. It also argues for periodic balance assertions against statements as an automated tripwire.
- Because Pilot and Docyt publish no metrics, an agent-operated local app should keep its own scoreboard: auto-posted vs proposed vs corrected counts per period, as Booke's "AI vs team" monthly report does. That builds calibrated trust.

### Gaps
- I found no specific regulator or standards-body guidance (AICPA, PCAOB, IRS) on AI-generated bookkeeping in these sources. The regulatory concern appears only as practitioner liability arguments.
- I found no verified first-hand Reddit threads (r/Accounting, r/Bookkeeping). Search returned aggregator and vendor-forum content instead.
- Bench's status in 2026 (still operating under Employer.com, quality of service) is not documented beyond Wikipedia's note on integration.

## 8. Concrete patterns an agent-operated local double-entry app should copy

### Takeaway
The converging design is: **every agent output is a proposal with provenance, evidence and confidence; policy decides what auto-posts; corrections become explicit rules; a single review inbox collects everything; the agent pre-fills a close checklist; every number is explainable with links back to lines; and everything is reversible and logged by actor.** Each pattern below is tied to a product that does it.

### Inferences (synthesized blueprint for the local app)
- **States**: `proposed` (agent draft, not in balances by default, with a toggle to preview "as if posted") → `posted` (in the journal) → `reversed` (via a reversing entry, never deleted). Add `needs_info` for items waiting on the user, like Puzzle's and QBO's client questions.
- **Per-proposal metadata**: actor (`agent`/`user`/`rule`/`import`), method (`rule` | `doc_match` | `memory` | `model`), confidence bucket, reason text, evidence refs (statement line id, document id, up to N similar past transactions, rule id), and the ledger version the agent read.
- **Autopost policy**: per operation (categorize, match, accrue, reverse-accrual, reclassify), per account and per amount ceiling. Default to propose-only, as with Xero's off-by-default. Let the user promote individual rules to auto.
- **Review inbox**: grouped by vendor/rule so one decision covers many lines (a defense against systematic errors), with bulk approve, inline edit, and keyboard navigation. Keyboard review is an opportunity, since no competitor documents it. "Approve and make rule" is the primary secondary action.
- **Close checklist**: the agent owns the steps (Collect, Book, Reconcile per account, Schedules, QC scan, Flux, Report). Each step shows status, the agent's evidence and a user sign-off. The agent adds tasks automatically when the QC scan finds anomalies.
- **Explain**: every report cell has an "Explain" action that runs a flux-style decomposition with links to lines, plus a stored snapshot reference.
- **Scoreboard and calibration**: show monthly auto-posted, proposed, accepted-as-is, corrected and reverted counts, so the user can tune thresholds with evidence.
- **Portability**: one-click export of journal, CoA, trial balance and documents in open formats.

### Gaps
- No product publicly documents a full end-to-end agent-operated UI where the agent and the human use the identical operation set. Rillet's MCP (read access with permissions) and Intuit Intelligence's prompt-to-task are partial analogues. Write-side semantics through MCP (whether agent writes are drafts) were not documented.
- No sources documented how products handle conflicts when a user edits an entry the agent has queued or is about to act on (concurrency, locks). A local app will need its own policy here.
